As the internet applications become omnipresent, log processing has become an essential component of the data pipeline. Kafka is a distributed messaging system that was developed at Linkedin for collecting and delivering high volumes of log data with low latency. It incorporates ideas from existing log aggregation and messaging systems.

There is normally huge amounts of log data produced, and it typically includes (1) user activity events corresponding to logins, pageviews, clicks, “likes”, sharing, comments, and search queries; (2) operational metrics such as service call stack, call latency, errors, and system metrics such as CPU, memory, network. There are many uses for processing this data such as providing relevant search experience, recommendations driven by item popularity, ad targetting, and security applications to identify abusive behaviors. 

The production systems that often creates this data is magnitudes of higher in size than the "real" data. China mobile collects 5-8TB of data every day. There are earlier systems built for processing log data, but they rely on scarping log files off of production servers. For example Facebook's Scribe or Yahoo's Data Highway, they are primarily designed for collecting and loading the log data for offline analysis such as into a Hadoop system. Some earlier messaging systems such as IBM Websphere MQ has transactional support that allow applications to write messages to queues atomically. But they do not focus on throughput as a primary design constraint.
At Linkedin, Kafka greatly simplifies their infrastructure, since they can exploit a single piece of software for both online and offline consumption of the log data of all types.
